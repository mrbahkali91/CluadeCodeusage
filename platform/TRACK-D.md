# Track D — Back-testing harness and data-quality monitoring

## Status

The two headline defects this document reported — a valuation band too wide to act on, and a
confidence score that ranked misses above hits — are **fixed as of `valuation-v2`**. The band is
27.9% of value at 71.7% coverage (both spec targets met, for the first time), and
Spearman(confidence, |error|) is −0.193 where it was +0.151. The corpus generator was made
heteroscedastic first, because the original one made the confidence question unanswerable in
principle. Details in "The finding that mattered, and how it was resolved".

## Read this before quoting any number below

**The transaction corpus is synthetic.** It is deterministic fixture data generated by
`sreoi_pipeline/seed.py` and registered under a source flagged `is_synthetic = True`. Every
figure in this document is therefore a measurement of the **engine's internal consistency** —
whether the estimator recovers a price drawn from the same generator as its evidence — and
**not** of accuracy against the Saudi market. Assumption A-01 (transaction-level Ministry of
Justice open data) is still unvalidated, so no real transaction data exists to test against.

Publishing "median absolute error 7.9%" as market accuracy would be the most misleading thing
this product could do, so the caveat is carried as **data**, not prose:

- `BacktestReport.evidence_is_synthetic` and `.caveat`, plus `measures: "internal_consistency"`,
  are top-level fields of every payload — before the metrics, so a client cannot render the
  numbers without receiving the provenance;
- `backtest_runs.evidence_is_synthetic` and `backtest_runs.caveat` are `NOT NULL` columns, so a
  stored run cannot outlive the knowledge of what it measured;
- the dashboard leads with a `role="alert"` banner, not a footnote;
- the CLI prints the caveat *before* the first metric, so a truncated copy-paste still carries it.

### Reproducing these numbers

They require a corpus generated by `heteroscedastic-v2`. An existing database keeps whatever
corpus it already has — `seed_transactions` is idempotent and returns 0 if transactions exist —
so a database seeded before this change will silently pair the **v1 corpus** with the v2
estimator and produce neither set of figures. Rebuild with `RESET=1 ./deploy-local.sh`, or check
`source_records.raw_payload->>'dispersion'` on the synthetic-fixture source.

### Confidence calibration (E4.4, spec §7 row 4)

152 valued cases. Two criteria are measured, and **the point-error criterion is now the primary
one** — see the note below on why the interval criterion cannot fairly judge an adaptive band.

| Metric | `valuation-v1` | **`valuation-v2`** | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spearman(confidence, \|error\|) | **+0.151** | **−0.193** | v1 had the **wrong sign** |
| Point-error AUC (within 12%) | 0.476 | **0.603** | v1 was at chance, v2 discriminates |
| Interval-hit AUC | **0.329** | 0.429 | still below 0.5 — structural, see below |
| Mean confidence, cases within 12% | — | **0.457** | correctly higher |
| Mean confidence, cases outside 12% | — | **0.415** | correctly lower |
| Mean claimed / realised (point) | 50.1% / 72.1% | 44.2% / 65.8% | under-states |

**The sign defect is fixed.** Under v1, a higher stated confidence went with a *larger* absolute
error, and the score ranked misses above hits — worse than a constant. Under v2 the ordering is
correct at every seed tested.

**The interval-hit AUC is not a defect to chase.** Sweeping the band quantile pair moves it
between 0.355 and 0.435 and it never reaches 0.5 for *any* pair, while point-error AUC stays
fixed at 0.603 throughout. The reason is structural: with an empirical-quantile band, the band
**narrows exactly when confidence rises** — both are driven by comparable agreement. A confident
case therefore states a tighter interval, which is intrinsically *less* likely to contain the
realised price. A confidence score cannot simultaneously predict "small error" and "interval
hit" while the band is adaptive; the two criteria are in tension by construction. Point error is
the criterion that describes what the score is for, so it is the one reported first.

**The level is deliberately left un-rescaled.** Confidence averages 44% against a 66% realised
hit rate, which looks like textbook under-confidence — but on this corpus the gap is not
calibration error. It is the score correctly reporting evidence deficiencies that are really
there:

| | Cost to confidence |
|---|---|
| NATIONAL-only index tier (`index_quality` 0.4 of 1.0) | −0.090 |
| Median `n_effective` 5.41 against the 12 treated as full | −0.137 |
| **Structural ceiling on this corpus**, at perfect agreement | **0.773** |

Median achieved confidence is 0.455 against that 0.773 ceiling. The realised 66% comes from the
*generator* being benign — an unbiased estimator against σ of 4.5–21% — not from the evidence
being strong. Fitting a scale factor to close the gap would erase a true signal in order to
match a synthetic distribution, and would be wrong on real data in an unknown direction.
Under-confidence is the safe direction: it over-refuses rather than over-promises. The level
must be re-fit on real registered transactions.

**Measured cost of that choice:** the share of live opportunities reading `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`
rose from 37.5% to **41.1%**, because v2 is correctly less confident where the evidence
genuinely disagrees. That is the price of an honest gate, and **district-level index coverage is
the highest-leverage fix** — worth up to +0.090 on every valuation, against a 0.60 gate.

### The fixture defect that made this measurable

The original generator drew every price from `gauss(1.0, 0.11)` — one dispersion for the entire
city. Under a generator like that the error on any held-out sale is drawn from the same
distribution regardless of its evidence, so **nothing observable can predict it** and no
confidence score can be calibrated against it however well built. That is why the v1 AUC of
0.329 was uninterpretable: it could not distinguish a broken estimator from a fixture with no
signal to find.

`heteroscedastic-v2` gives each district three longitude-banded sub-zones with σ from 0.045 to
0.215, plus a mild area term (unusual sizes trade in thinner markets). The zones are spatially
coherent, so comparables found near a subject mostly share its zone — which is precisely how the
estimator gets to *observe* local dispersion. Confidence can now be right or wrong about it,
which is the point. The harness immediately picked the structure up: Al Rimal (highest σ) and Al
Munsiyah are the two segments flagged for an over-wide band, and they are the two the generator
makes hardest.

The corpus remains **synthetic**, and every caveat at the top of this document still applies.
A heteroscedastic fixture makes calibration *measurable*; it does not make it *validated*.

## The finding that mattered, and how it was resolved

**Both headline defects are fixed as of `valuation-v2`.** This section is kept as the record of
what was wrong and why, because the reasoning is the useful part and the numbers above are
meaningless without it.

### 1. The band was honest but useless — RESOLVED

The v1 band covered 98.7% of realised prices against a 70% target, and was **58.8% of value
wide**. A band spanning −28%/+28% cannot distinguish "20% below market" from "fairly priced",
which is the single decision the product exists to support: the §5.1 discount normalisation
awards 50/100 at a 10% discount and 80/100 at 20%, and both sat comfortably inside the band.

This was a **specification defect, not an implementation bug**. §2.2 was implemented exactly as
written; the arithmetic of the formula at the effective sample sizes the engine achieves is what
produced the result:

| | Width | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted 25th–75th percentile, no inflation | 13.6% | 40.3% |
| After `spread_factor = 1 + 0.6/√n_eff` on *both* ends | 58.8% | 98.7% |

Median `n_effective` is **5.41** — less than half the 12 the confidence formula treats as full
evidence — so the spread factor sat at ~1.26 and, applied to both ends, inflated the band by a
factor of 4.3. The design offered no setting between 40% and 99% coverage.

**Resolution.** The inflation term is gone and the band is now an empirical weighted quantile
pair. The pair was chosen by sweeping it against both spec targets rather than by argument; of
seven candidates, exactly one satisfies coverage ≥ 70% **and** width ≤ 30%:

| Pair | Width | Coverage | med \|e\| | point AUC | Both targets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 / 0.75 | 12.0% | 40.1% | 7.04% | 0.603 | no |
| 0.20 / 0.80 | 14.5% | 49.3% | 7.04% | 0.603 | no |
| 0.15 / 0.85 | 18.5% | 61.2% | 7.04% | 0.603 | no |
| 0.10 / 0.90 | 21.9% | 67.1% | 7.04% | 0.603 | no |
| **0.05 / 0.95** | **27.9%** | **71.7%** | **7.04%** | **0.603** | **yes** |
| 0.02 / 0.98 | 30.6% | 76.3% | 7.04% | 0.603 | no |
| 0.00 / 1.00 | 34.0% | 77.6% | 7.04% | 0.603 | no |

Two things the sweep makes plain. The point estimate and the confidence score are untouched by
the choice — median error and point AUC are constant down the table — so this is purely a
decision about how to *state* uncertainty. And v1's own recommendation (Q10/Q90 plus a shrunken
inflation term) would **not** have met both targets: Q10/Q90 covers only 67.1%. The sweep was
worth running instead of trusting the recommendation.

### 2. The confidence figure was inverted — RESOLVED

v1's AUC of 0.329 and Spearman of **+0.151** meant the score ranked misses above hits: a higher
stated confidence went with a *larger* absolute error. This was precisely the failure the
specification names — *"a confidence score that is not calibrated is worse than none, because it
invites trust it has not earned."*

The hypothesis recorded here was that `mean(w)`, worth 0.25 of the formula, measures how
*similar* the comparables are to the subject rather than how much they *agree with each other*,
which is what predicts error. **That hypothesis was correct.** v2 weights agreement at 0.40 and
similarity at 0.10, inverting the two, and the sign inverts with them: Spearman **−0.193**,
point-error AUC **0.603**, and mean confidence 0.457 on cases within 12% against 0.415 on cases
outside it.

The second hypothesis — that confidence rises with `n_effective` while the band *narrows* with
it, so the two move oppositely with respect to coverage — was also correct, and is now
understood to be **inherent to any adaptive band** rather than a bug. See the interval-AUC note
in the calibration section.

Fixing this required fixing the fixture first: the original homoscedastic generator made the
question unanswerable. That work is described above.

### 3. No valuation ever claimed more than 70% confidence — UNCHANGED, and understood

The 70–100% buckets remain empty, and this is now quantified rather than suspected: the
structural ceiling on this corpus is **0.773**, of which 0.090 is lost to the NATIONAL-only index
tier and 0.137 to an achieved `n_effective` of 5.41 against the 12 treated as full. The score is
not being pessimistic; the evidence really is thin. This is the structural reason **41.1% of live
opportunities read `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`**. District-level index coverage remains the
highest-leverage fix.

### 4. Refusal is driven by weight concentration, not comparable scarcity — UNCHANGED

Of 25 refusals, only 2 had fewer than 8 comparables; the rest had 8–31. The multiplicative kernel
product (correctly) lets one mismatched dimension veto a comparable, but the consequence is that
Kish `n_effective` collapses even with double-digit counts, and `MIN_EFFECTIVE_N = 3` then
refuses. The refusals are *correct* — the evidence really is concentrated in a few rows — but the
message ("insufficient comparable evidence: 14 comparables, effective n=2.61") will read to an
analyst as a contradiction. Still worth a UI wording decision, and still worth asking whether the
veto bandwidths are too tight.

### 5. Bias: the engine under-values — IMPROVED, not eliminated

Median signed error moved from **−2.55% to −0.98%**. Still in the safe direction
(under-valuing shrinks apparent discounts rather than inflating them) and still systematic. The
likely cause is unchanged: time adjustment uses the NATIONAL index, which does not track the
4.5%/year district drift the generator applies. On real data this must be re-measured before it
is dismissed — in a rising market, under-correcting is the direction that *flatters* discounts.

## Defects found in code Track D does not own

**a. `ComparableRepository.find` filters `transacted_on <= as_of`.** Correct for valuing a live
opportunity today; **leaks in any as-of evaluation**, because the inclusive bound admits
same-day sales — including, in a back-test, the held-out sale itself. The harness therefore does
not call it. `sreoi_pipeline.backtest.find_evidence_before` mirrors it exactly (importing
`RADIUS_STEPS_M`, `LOOKBACK_MONTHS`, `AREA_LOWER_FACTOR`, `AREA_UPPER_FACTOR` so the two stay in
step) with two deliberate differences: a strict `<`, and exclusion of the subject by id. On top
of that, the pure `sreoi_domain.backtest.check_no_leakage` re-checks every returned row before
it reaches the estimator, so a future change to the SQL cannot silently invalidate the numbers.
`tests/test_backtest.py::TestLeakageGuard` (5 tests) and
`test_evidence_query_never_returns_the_subject_or_the_future` fail if leakage is reintroduced
from either side. **Recommendation for the coordinator:** if any as-of feature is built (what-if
pricing, historical replay), give the repository an explicit `inclusive: bool` parameter rather
than letting each caller rediscover this.

**b. `properties.merged_into_id` is never populated.** The ingest path resolves onto an existing
property *before* creating a row, so there is nothing to mark merged: 25 `AUTO_MERGE` decisions,
0 rows with `merged_into_id` set. Any duplicate-rate metric built on that column is structurally
zero and would report a healthy system forever. `sreoi_pipeline.quality.duplicate_resolution`
reads the decision log instead, and says so in its payload `note`. The column is not wrong — it
records a retrospective merge of two already-stored rows — but nothing performs that operation
yet.

**c. Verification checks accumulate per re-evaluation with no batch identifier.** `verification_checks`
is append-only, which is right, but there is no run id, so "the current verdict" has to be
reconstructed as *the newest row per `check_type`*. My first implementation used a 5-second
time window instead and reported a **phantom 30.4% agent-vs-deterministic disagreement rate** —
the corpus loader re-evaluates several opportunities inside the same second, so the window summed
their batches. That was my defect, found by disbelieving the number and fixed before it shipped;
it is recorded here because the underlying schema gap will bite the next person the same way. A
`verification_run_id` column would remove the guesswork.

**Design notes worth knowing.** Refusals are counted in the denominator of `cases` and reported
by grouped cause, never dropped. Segments below `min_segment_cases = 3` are suppressed rather
than reported as fact. Runs are reproducible from `(seed, sample, min_history_days)` and stored
append-only with the whole report in JSONB, so the page and the API can never disagree. The
dashboard signals status with a glyph **and** a word **and** a colour (WCAG 2.1 AA 1.4.1),
asserted by `test_status_is_never_signalled_by_colour_alone`, which parses every pill.

## Left undone

1. **No Alembic migration**, as instructed. The three tables reach the database through
   `Base.metadata` (`models_quality.py` is auto-imported by `model_modules.load_all()`). After
   pulling this, run
   `.venv/bin/python -c "from sreoi_persistence.db import get_engine; from sreoi_persistence.models import Base; Base.metadata.create_all(get_engine())"`
   until the coordinator writes the consolidated migration. Note the four CHECK constraints on
   the new tables — they must survive into it, particularly
   `ck_backtest_result_valued_has_prediction` / `..._refusal_has_no_prediction`, which make a
   refusal and a prediction mutually exclusive at the database level.
2. **Auction-outcome tracking** (spec §7 row 3) is **not built**. Nothing in the schema records
   an opening price or a hammer price, and there is no auction outcome data to record; inventing
   a table with no producer would have been worse than the gap. It needs an `auction_outcomes`
   table (opening price, our estimate at the time, final hammer price, lot reference) written by
   an Infath ingestion path that does not exist yet. This is the dataset the architecture calls a
   moat, so it should be scheduled as soon as any auction feed lands.
3. **Analyst blind review** (spec §7 row 2) is a human process, out of scope for a harness.
4. **No nav link to `/admin/quality`.** `base.html` is not mine to edit. The string
   `nav.quality` is registered in both locales and ready; the coordinator adds one `<a>`.
5. **Snapshots are not scheduled.** `python -m sreoi_pipeline.quality` is the cron entry point;
   nothing invokes it periodically, so `quality_snapshots` currently holds only manual readings
   and the drift comparison on the dashboard has a short history.
6. **Thresholds are calibrated against a synthetic corpus** and should be revisited on real
   data. `MAX_USEFUL_INTERVAL_WIDTH_PCT = 0.30` in particular is my derivation from the discount
   normalisation table, not a figure from the specification, and is documented as such at its
   definition.
7. **No rental/yield back-test**: the rental engine (E4.6) is not built.
8. **Segment sample sizes are small** (25–58 per district/area band). Coverage differences
   between segments are within noise at this size; the width failure is not.
